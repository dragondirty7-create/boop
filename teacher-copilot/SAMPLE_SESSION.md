# EEC Teacher Copilot — Session plan

**Prototype for adult review; not market-ready.**
Content: eec-teacher-copilot-0.1.0 · Draft — EEC human review pending

## Create a two-pitch phrase
Explorers · ages 8–10 · 25 minutes · Keyboard / piano
Offer turns or paired echoes; a listening turn is valid.

### Preparation
The adult chooses two comfortable keys, one higher and one lower.
Aim for 70% familiar/review musical attempts and 30% new attempts. This is a teaching intention, not a measured outcome or time split. Weave review into Make.
Offer breaks whenever requested; shorten repeats to preserve Make and Land.

Teacher owns pacing, access, sound levels, breaks, and stopping. No learner account, recording, upload, or automated safety service is included.

### Five-part session

#### Launch · 3 min
Copy a familiar four-count pulse. Hear it, then sing, tap, move, play, or point.
Teacher phrase: “Listen, then try your way.”

#### Play · 4 min
Echo Clap: Copy a familiar four-count pulse. Switch roles so the adult echoes a group-made phrase.
Teacher phrase: “Your turn. I will copy you.”

#### Learn · 7 min
Hear a two-pitch phrase; sing or play it back before naming high and low.
Teacher phrase: “Listen first. Then try one change.”

#### Make · 8 min
Choose an order for two comfortable pitches. Repeat the phrase, then swap one pitch.
Teacher phrase: “Make your phrase. Choose one thing to change.”

#### Land · 3 min
Offer an optional musical share. Ask: “What would you play again?” Pointing or listening is welcome. End without pressure.
Teacher phrase: “Play again, point, or finish.”

### Access choices
- Sound level — Quiet: Use soft taps or humming, one sound at a time. Check comfort; reduce or stop sound on request.
- Language — Short and concrete: Model first; give one short instruction at a time.
- Response time — No timer: Use no response countdown. The teacher may repeat, skip, or stop at any point.
- Notation — Simple icons: After hearing and playing, offer four large dots for counts; a blank dot can represent silence.
- Movement — Seated: Offer seated taps or pointing; keep materials within comfortable reach.
- Theme — Music: Let the group choose a sound or phrase.
- Visual density — Calm: Show one step at a time with no animation or decorative clutter.

### Look for musical evidence
A two-pitch phrase was made, repeated, and changed. This is an observation prompt, not a recorded result.

### Off-screen extension
With a responsible adult, repeat the phrase off screen using comfortable taps, humming, or an available instrument. Invent one small change.

### Facilitation and handoff
- Ready for the next move: “Keep that phrase. Change one sound, then listen.” Model one change; offer copy, point, or listen. Stop option: pause or end immediately; no completion is required.
- Needs another way in: “Listen to mine. Try one sound, or point.” Use a slower pulse and a shorter pattern. Offer a repeat or a break. Stop option: pause or end immediately; no completion is required.
- Waiting for a response: “Would you like to listen, try, or take a break?” Wait without a countdown; model once if invited. Do not infer why someone is quiet. Stop option: pause or end immediately; no completion is required.
- Wants to stop: “We can stop. Your music can wait.” End the activity without pressure; an optional quiet share is enough. Stop option: pause or end immediately; no completion is required.
- Safety concern reported by adult: “Pause the music. A responsible adult needs to help.” Follow your organization’s safeguarding or emergency procedure. This tool cannot assess danger, monitor safety, or send alerts. Stop option: pause or end immediately; no completion is required.
